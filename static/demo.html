Container orchestration in the age of the very large cluster

By A. Reporter · Technology

Operators running clusters past a few thousand nodes describe the same pattern: the scheduler stops being the bottleneck and the control plane's write path takes over. What used to be a capacity question becomes a consistency question.

The workarounds are familiar to anyone who has run a large database. Shard the control plane, relax read consistency where the workload tolerates it, and accept that some operations will be eventually consistent whether or not that is written down anywhere.

Advertisement

None of this is new. What is new is how many organisations now operate at a scale where it matters, and how little of the tooling assumes it.

The uncomfortable conclusion is that most teams reach for orchestration long before they reach the scale that justifies it, and pay the operational cost the whole way.
